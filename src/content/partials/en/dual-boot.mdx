To install Linux alongside Windows instead of replacing it, prepare Windows first:

1. **Back up important files.** Resizing partitions rarely fails, but it can.
2. **Find your BitLocker recovery key.** With device encryption on, Windows may ask for it after the boot order changes. Look it up on your [Microsoft account’s recovery key page](https://account.microsoft.com/devices/recoverykey) or suspend BitLocker for the install.
3. **Turn off Fast Startup.** Control Panel → Power Options → Choose what the power buttons do → uncheck **Turn on fast startup**. Otherwise Windows doesn’t fully close its partition, which can cause problems.
4. **Free up space for Linux.** Right-click the Start button, open **Disk Management**, right-click drive C:, and choose **Shrink Volume**. Leave at least 30 GB unallocated, 60 GB or more if you can.

<Callout type="note">
  The installer uses this unallocated space when you choose an option such as **Install alongside Windows** or **Use free space**.
</Callout>
