CentOS Stream is built by Red Hat Enterprise Linux (RHEL) engineers. It is derived from Fedora Linux and is the branch from which upcoming RHEL minor releases are cut; the project calls it the midstream between Fedora and RHEL. Within a major version, updates are delivered continuously as they are ready. The newest major version is CentOS Stream <Latest slug="centos-stream" />.

### Highlights

- **Just ahead of RHEL.** Changes meant for the next RHEL minor release land here first, which makes CentOS Stream a way to preview where RHEL is going.
- **A new major version every three years, maintained for five.** Each major version is maintained until the end of the matching RHEL release's Full Support phase, about five years. The newest one runs until <Latest slug="centos-stream" field="eol" />.
- **RHEL development in the open.** The sources live on GitLab and anyone can propose changes. The RHEL engineering team reviews them, because a contribution to CentOS Stream is a contribution to RHEL.
- **A base for SIGs.** CentOS Special Interest Groups build variant editions and images on top of it and package additional software.

### Editions and variants

- Official images are the installation DVD ISO, GenericCloud images for your own cloud, Amazon EC2 images, container images and Vagrant boxes.
- Alternative ISOs, including live images for trying a desktop, come from the Alternative Images SIG and are rebuilt at least every three months.

### Who it suits

- Developers and vendors who want to test software or hardware against the next RHEL minor release early
- People who want to contribute fixes or features directly to RHEL
- SIG members packaging newer technology on an RHEL-family base

### Good to know

- Unlike the old CentOS Linux, it is not a rebuild of RHEL. It runs ahead of RHEL, so it is not identical to any particular RHEL minor release.
- Support ends with RHEL's Full Support phase, about five years in, after which you need to move to a new major version.
- On x86_64, CentOS Stream 10 needs an x86-64-v3 CPU. It drops the Xorg server in favor of Wayland, with Xwayland for older X11 apps, and leaves out desktop apps such as GIMP, LibreOffice and Inkscape; install those from Flathub or EPEL.

### A short history

CentOS Stream first appeared alongside CentOS 8 in September 2019. In December 2020 the CentOS Project announced it was moving its focus from CentOS Linux, the RHEL rebuild, to CentOS Stream, and CentOS Linux 8 ended at the close of 2021.
