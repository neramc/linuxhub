Fedora Linux is built by a worldwide community and sponsored by Red Hat. A new version arrives roughly every six months, and each one receives updates until about a month after the release two versions later (around 13 months). Fedora adopts new open-source technology early — new kernels, GNOME, system tooling — and much of what matures here later flows into CentOS Stream and Red Hat Enterprise Linux.

The flagship **Workstation** edition ships a largely unmodified GNOME desktop, and **KDE Plasma Desktop** is an official edition too. Other desktops such as Xfce and Cinnamon come as “Spins”, and image-based (atomic) variants like **Silverblue** and **Kinoite** update the whole system as a unit. System packages are managed with `dnf`, desktop apps with Flatpak.

It suits developers and everyday users who want current software without giving up stability. Because of patent concerns, some media codecs and proprietary drivers such as NVIDIA’s aren’t included by default; add a third-party repository like RPM Fusion if you need them.
